# Keeping it simple

A standing rule for every system: the game must never turn into spreadsheet management. Depth comes from decisions that matter, not from chores that repeat.

## The resource rule

Resources are money-like, not a supply chain puzzle.

- **Gold is the currency.** Everything is priced in it.
- **Other ores are worth a fraction of gold and can be spent as if they were gold.** Silver is a fraction of gold, copper and iron less again. A player holding iron is holding money, at a discount.
- This means a player never has to convert, trade or plan around a resource they do not want. Surplus is always useful.
- Physical movement still matters: goods must be where they are needed. What is simplified is the arithmetic, not the logistics.
- Special resources stay special. Explosives, missiles, fuel and uranium are used for specific things and cannot be spent as money, because those are the decisions worth having.

## What must be automatic

| Chore | Handled by |
| --- | --- |
| Feeding construction sites with materials | Convoy dispatch, automatically |
| Restocking air defence with missiles | Troops restock their own defences |
| Choosing a route for an army | Cheapest route unless told otherwise |
| Upgrading civilian buildings | Civilians do it themselves over time |
| Rebalancing stockpiles between towns | The dispatcher |
| Recruiting to a target garrison | An auto-recruit setting |

The player's job is to set intent (build here, hold this, research that) and the systems carry it out.

## What must stay manual

These are the decisions the game is about, so they never get automated:

- Where to attack and with how much.
- Where to place government, military and infrastructure.
- What to research next, and when to push for the next era.
- Diplomacy, factions and treaties.
- Whether to spend the explosives on a mountain or the missiles on a defence.

## Interface rules that follow from this

1. **Anything you do more than five times a session gets a bulk action.** The bulk upgrade menu is the model.
2. **Every list has a sort and a filter, and remembers them.**
3. **Nothing important is buried more than two taps deep.**
4. **Every number can explain itself in one line.**
5. **No panel requires reading to use it for the first time.** If it does, it needs a default.
6. **Overlays are off by default** and toggled from one place.
