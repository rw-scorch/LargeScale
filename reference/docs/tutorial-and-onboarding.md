# Tutorial and onboarding

The game has a lot of systems. The way to keep it approachable is not to explain them all, but to teach one rule at each moment the player first needs it, and to make sure nothing important is invisible.

## The rules a new player must be told

These are the ones nobody can work out on their own, because they happen while the player is not looking:

1. **The world keeps running when you leave.** Your economy, building and research carry on.
2. **While you are offline, you produce at your online average minus 10 percent.** So a session spent setting things up pays off overnight, and a session spent idle does not.
3. **Your troops defend 5 percent weaker while you are away.** Being offline is a small penalty, not a shield.
4. **Nobody can attack while the world is asleep.** Attacks only happen when someone is connected.
5. **You can see the whole map.** There is no fog. What you cannot see is what someone is planning.
6. **Fighting near a city core is harder.** Attack and defence both rise closer to a core, and defence rises faster.
7. **Terrain is not permanent.** Mountains can be blown open by engineers, and the gap can be filled back in.
8. **Ammunition is physical.** Air defence that is cut off from supply stops firing.
9. **Gold is the money. Other ores are worth a fraction of it and can be spent the same way.**
10. **You will be told when something is aimed at you.** Alerts are automatic.

## Shape of the tutorial

Not a separate mode. A first world with a guide layer on top, so everything learned is real progress.

**Step 1, spawning.** Pick a spot. The game shows good spawn regions and explains that terrain decides how fast you move and how hard you are to attack.

**Step 2, first land.** Split a stack, send it, watch it take ground. One sentence: troops are a number, stacks are how you move them.

**Step 3, first town.** Paint a residential zone. Civilians build. One sentence: you place government, military and infrastructure, and civilians do the rest.

**Step 4, first food.** Place a farm, see food arrive, see the population grow. Introduce the needs panel.

**Step 5, first road.** Build a road to a second town and watch a convoy move. Introduce goods being physical.

**Step 6, first research.** Queue an upgrade, and say that research continues while you are away.

**Step 7, logging off.** This is the important one. Before the first log off, show the offline card:

> While you are away your economy runs at your online average minus 10 percent, your troops defend 5 percent weaker, and nobody can attack until someone is connected. Set your stacks to hold or fall back before you go.

**Step 8, coming back.** The "while you were away" summary is itself a teaching moment: what grew, what was built, what was attacked.

**Step 9, first neighbour.** When borders touch, explain war declarations and the notice period.

**Step 10, first defence.** When the first enemy aircraft or missile appears, explain coverage, ammunition and reloading.

Each step is a card with one sentence, one picture and a dismiss. All steps are repeatable from a help menu, and skippable in one tap for the second world.

## Ongoing help, not a wall of text

- **Hover and long-press explain everything.** Every number in the interface has a one-line source, for example "food: 12 per minute, from 3 farms, winter penalty applied".
- **The to-do panel is a tutorial that never ends.** Idle stacks, buildings ready to upgrade, research finished with nothing queued, a storage node that is full.
- **Warnings before mistakes, not after.** Grey out an action and say why, rather than letting it fail.
- **No separate glossary page.** The ten rules are taught in place and repeatable from the help menu, and every number explains itself where it appears.
- **No head start.** Everyone begins from the same position, so the tutorial teaches rather than gives.

## What the tutorial must avoid

- No forced sequence that blocks play. If the player wants to go and fight, let them.
- No text longer than two sentences per card.
- No teaching of systems that are not yet unlocked. Air defence explanations appear in the Industrial era, not at spawn.
